Where the line is

What it does, and what it never does

The distinction is the whole design, so it leads, not the fine print. Every PDF report carries a footer stating that it is observations only, contains no treatment recommendations, and is not a diagnosis.

Never

  • Calculates or suggests an insulin dose, carbohydrate ratio, correction factor, or basal setting
  • Diagnoses any condition
  • Predicts a future glucose value, or issues real-time alarms
  • Tells a patient to change their therapy

Does

  • Computes the consensus CGM metrics
  • Surfaces ranked, time-stamped past patterns
  • Writes a plain-language question the patient brings to you
  • Re-measures the same metric after a wellness change the patient chose to try

Methodology

Deterministic, on-device, auditable

Patterns are computed by a fixed, rules-based engine that runs on the patient's phone. The same data always produces the same report. Glucose is never sent to a large-language model or any third party. There is no generative step in the analysis, and so nothing to hallucinate.

The metrics follow the standard consensus definitions, with the Glucose Management Indicator computed as GMI = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean mg/dL and CV = SD / mean × 100%. The cutpoints and the GMI constant are pinned as unit-tested constants, so the app and the PDF use the same definitions.

What it cannot see: it has no insulin, pump, or meal data unless the patient supplies it, and it does not infer dosing. When CGM coverage is too sparse to summarize, it says so rather than guessing.

Evidence

Clinical reference points cite a primary source

Each reference point is tied to a primary citation with the population noted (type 1 vs. general). The consensus targets and the GMI definition follow the international CGM consensus (Battelino et al., Diabetes Care, 2019) and the Glucose Management Indicator (Bergenstal et al., Diabetes Care, 2018), alongside the current ADA Standards of Care.

Population and edition are noted on each so you can confirm the source supports the exact claim.

In practice

How your patients use it

It reads CGM glucose from Apple Health (Dexcom G6 and G7, Eversense, Stelo) or from a file export (FreeStyle Libre 2 and 3 from LibreView, Medtronic from CareLink), with no logging, and turns it into the profile above plus one focused question and this one-pager. The intent is narrow: the 15-minute visit can start at the decision, not the data review.

When a patient tries a general wellness change, the app shows the same metric before and after. It is an association, n-of-1, not adjusted for confounders; it describes what changed or differed, never what "worked," and it never contradicts your plan.

Data, privacy & security

The liability question, answered plainly

Glucose is read from Apple Health or from a CareLink or LibreView file export, and analyzed on the patient's device; it is never sent to a large-language model and is not stored on Eating Glasses servers. Data in transit and at rest is encrypted, records are row-level isolated per account, the patient controls all sharing, and account deletion is available in-app. Optional food scanning is separate: a food photo, voice note or typed description goes to an Eating Glasses server and to Google Gemini to estimate nutrition, and glucose is not part of that request.

HIPAA does not apply to a direct-to-consumer app like Eating Glasses, so Eating Glasses does not claim it. Eating Glasses is built to meet the requirements of the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and the Washington My Health My Data Act.
